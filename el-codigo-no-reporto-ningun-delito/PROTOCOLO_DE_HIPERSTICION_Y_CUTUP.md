# PROTOCOL OF HYPERSTITION AND CUT-UP

## How a claim acquires enough consequences to become its own evidence

**Status:** governing conceptual and literary document · version 0.1  
**Finished-work language:** English  
**Operations:** circulation, prediction, citation, cut-up, reattribution, retroactive causality

---

## 1. Central proposition

Provenance does not merely describe the object. Under sufficient circulation, provenance produces the object it claims to describe.

The disputed work begins as an uncertain relation among a signature, a link, a photograph and a name. Institutions repeat that relation for different local reasons. Repetition produces documentation. Documentation produces custody, conservation, price and scholarship. Those consequences become evidence that the relation must have preceded them.

> The claim was not true because it described the past.  
> It became difficult to call false because it organized the future.

This is the hyperstitional engine of the work.

---

## 2. What hyperstition means here

Hyperstition is not occult decoration, a mystical blockchain or a vague idea that fiction “comes alive.” It is a demonstrable feedback process:

```text
CLAIM
  ↓
CIRCULATION
  ↓
INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE
  ↓
MATERIAL CONSEQUENCE
  ↓
NEW DOCUMENTATION
  ↓
RETROSPECTIVE EVIDENCE FOR THE CLAIM
```

Example:

1. Someone proposes that the blue form in the photograph is the disputed work.
2. The Archive creates a provisional description.
3. The Market cites the Archive.
4. A high-price sale funds conservation.
5. Conservation reconstructs blue behavior.
6. A museum exhibits the reconstruction.
7. Witnesses encounter it and refine older memories.
8. Later scholarship cites the witnesses, exhibition and reconstruction.
9. The original proposal now appears supported by an independent historical field.

No single step needs to contain a direct lie.

---

## 3. The crime as feedback

The crime cannot be located only at the beginning. It is distributed through the loop that converts a possibility into infrastructure.

Possible criminal moments:

- first attaching the work's identifier to the signature;
- choosing the photograph as evidence;
- removing a question mark from a date;
- publishing the first collector's biography;
- financing the circular sale;
- designating a reconstruction as original;
- citing a provisional source as settled history;
- repeating the claim after its source disappears;
- investigating it in a form that increases its authority.

The work refuses to choose one moment because the category of crime depends on where the observer interrupts the circuit.

---

## 4. The two manufactured entities

### 4.1 The artwork

The work may have emerged in this order:

1. a historical address;
2. a valid but ambiguous message;
3. a link;
4. a record of the link;
5. a need to explain what the link had indicated;
6. a reconstructed file;
7. a title;
8. an exhibition history;
9. an object capable of appearing to have caused the record.

> First there was a link.  
> Then there was a record of the link.  
> Then a work was required to explain what the record had been pointing to.

### 4.2 The investigator

The investigator may have emerged in the same manner:

1. unattributed analytical notes exist;
2. the notes are classified as investigative;
3. investigative notes imply an investigator;
4. the Continuity Office issues a credential;
5. citations produce a professional history;
6. expense records produce travel and a body;
7. a signature produces responsibility;
8. the accumulated documents begin using “I.”

> The investigator is a pronoun that acquired sufficient documentation to become a person.

The artwork and investigator are not identical mysteries. They are parallel products of the same documentary machine.

---

## 5. The visitor as third manufacture

The visitor enters without authored speech. The interface records actions:

- opened;
- accepted;
- sold;
- preserved;
- designated;
- abandoned;
- returned.

The system assembles these verbs into a first-person statement. A route becomes a biography of method.

The visitor therefore witnesses a self being manufactured from procedural traces.

At the second loop:

> You are not identified by an account.  
> You are identified by the consistency of your consequences.

---

## 6. Burroughs as procedure

The project may draw from broad procedural ideas associated with cut-up writing, textual contagion and language as control. It should not imitate the recognizable prose of a particular author.

Relevant operations:

- text behaves materially;
- a sentence can be cut, transported and reassembled;
- repetition changes authority;
- control operates through available commands;
- recording technologies alter the events they claim to preserve;
- fragments can reveal hidden continuities or manufacture false ones;
- syntax can carry institutional power independently of intention.

The browser allows these ideas to become executable literary form.

---

## 7. Cut-up is not randomness

Random recombination would weaken the evidentiary logic. Every cut must have:

- an agent or mechanism;
- an economic cause;
- a grammatical consequence;
- a provenance history;
- a later opportunity for recognition.

The system may use seeded variation to choose among authored cuts, but it must not create meaningless word salad.

> A cut is an event in the custody of a sentence.

---

## 8. Units that may be cut

### Noun

Removing a noun destabilizes the object or person being discussed.

> The Archive received the ________ in 2007.

The noun may reappear as a catalog title or button.

### Verb

Removing a verb transfers agency.

> The Office ________ the attribution.

The verb may become an action available to the visitor.

### Modifier

Removing words such as “possibly,” “alleged,” “current” or “available” increases certainty.

> the earliest **available** version  
> the earliest version

Modifiers are among the most valuable units in the Market.

### Pronoun

Removing or changing a pronoun transfers responsibility.

> I established the date.  
> We established the date.  
> The date was established.

### Citation

Removing a citation allows a sentence to travel faster while hiding dependence.

### Punctuation

A question mark, parentheses or quotation marks can be purchased separately. Their loss may convert uncertainty, qualification or reported speech into assertion.

### Whitespace

A gap can survive longer than the word removed from it. Empty space becomes evidence that an operation occurred.

---

## 9. Custody chain of a word

Every mobile word can carry a hidden or inspectable record:

```text
WORD: preserved
SOURCE: Archive statement C090
EXTRACTED BY: route action 14
HELD BY: visitor
USED IN: PRESERVE CURRENT ATTRIBUTION
QUOTED BY: Continuity Office report
CURRENT STATUS: CANONIZED
```

This chain permits the reader to discover that a familiar institutional verb came from a damaged witness statement or uncertain report.

The word's spelling remains stable while its ethical function changes.

---

## 10. Host model

A sentence fragment can inhabit different textual hosts.

### Witness host

Uncertain, sensory, situated.

> I think the screen went blue.

### Investigative host

Qualified and comparative.

> A witness recalls a blue screen.

### Archival host

Taxonomic and persistent.

> Blue-screen event documented.

### Market host

Scarce, characteristic, proprietary.

> The iconic blue-screen behavior.

### Interface host

Operational.

> ESTABLISH BLUE-SCREEN BEHAVIOR

### Visitor host

Biographical and accountable.

> I established the blue-screen behavior.

The phrase does not merely change style. Each host grants it new powers.

---

## 11. Viral fitness

Certain fragments are more likely to survive because they are:

- short;
- quotable;
- institutionally useful;
- compatible with metadata;
- capable of losing qualification;
- emotionally vivid;
- attached to price;
- easy to detach from source.

Long, careful sentences are less portable. The economy therefore favors compression.

The work should make this loss perceptible:

> Complex evidence dies young.  
> Its summary inherits the estate.

---

## 12. Mutation rules

A mobile phrase may undergo only authored transformations.

### Qualification loss

> may have been shown → was shown

### Agent loss

> the Archive assigned → was assigned

### Source promotion

> a witness recalls → records establish

### Temporal hardening

> around 1998 → in 1998 → 1998

### Ownership conversion

> held by → belonging to → by

### Market compression

> uncertain early relation → rare early work

### Institutional pluralization

> I believe → we understand

### Interface imperative

> the source was removed → REMOVE SOURCE

Each mutation must be reversible only by recovering the missing grammatical material.

---

## 13. Hyperstitional stages

Every important claim may pass through seven stages.

### H0 — Latent

A possible relation is visible but unnamed.

### H1 — Proposed

A voice states the relation with uncertainty.

### H2 — Recorded

The relation receives an identifier in TRACE.

### H3 — Priced

The Market assigns value to accepting or contesting it.

### H4 — Operational

Interfaces and institutions act as though it were true.

### H5 — Materialized

The claim produces an exhibition, reconstruction, payment or conserved object.

### H6 — Retrospective

The consequence is cited as evidence that the claim was true earlier.

### H7 — Canonical

The claim becomes free public context. Its origins become expensive specialist knowledge.

The visitor can accelerate, interrupt or profit from these transitions.

---

## 14. TRACE as engine

**TRACE — Transfer Record for Attribution, Continuity and Evidence** records the order in which claims refer to one another.

TRACE does not determine whether a relation is true. It gives the relation:

- a stable identifier;
- a visible predecessor;
- a current custodian;
- an institutional timestamp;
- a citation count;
- a status.

Once recorded, a relation can be cited without being restated. This increases circulation while reducing access to its original uncertainty.

Conceptual contradiction:

> TRACE records the provenance of claims.  
> Its records are then cited as provenance of the work.

---

## 15. Prophecy mechanics

Some documents describe future conditions in language that later appears historical.

Example:

Early conservation proposal:

> The work will have been shown in a form compatible with the reconstructed behavior.

Later catalog:

> The work was shown in a form compatible with the reconstructed behavior.

The future perfect is especially important: it allows a desired future to write the grammar of a completed past.

Possible interface verbs:

> RECORD AS FORTHCOMING  
> FUND EXPECTED DISCOVERY  
> INSURE FUTURE ORIGINAL  
> CITE UPON OCCURRENCE

---

## 16. Documents that create their referents

### Exhibition checklist

Listing a work produces the institutional need to locate or reconstruct it.

### Conservation plan

Describing essential behavior produces the behavior that later counts as original.

### Insurance schedule

Naming an asset produces a governable object of loss.

### Catalog essay

Connecting motifs creates an oeuvre capable of explaining the connection.

### Investigation report

Describing a pattern creates a citable historical interpretation.

### Visitor route

Recording choices produces an independent researcher capable of having made them.

---

## 17. Interface as control grammar

The interface controls less through prohibitions than through its available verbs.

If the reader can only:

> ACCEPT  
> ESTABLISH  
> DESIGNATE  
> PRESERVE  
> REMOVE

then even skeptical actions produce institutional outputs.

Resistance requires other verbs:

> WITHHOLD  
> RETURN  
> LEAVE UNRESOLVED  
> REFUSE ATTRIBUTION  
> KEEP IN CONTEXT  
> DECLINE TO CITE  
> LISTEN AGAIN

The work must offer these without presenting them as morally pure escape routes.

---

## 18. Cut-up events

### Event A — Verb extraction

A button steals a verb and gains its agency.

### Event B — Pronoun transfer

The investigator's “I” migrates into the visitor's assembled statement.

### Event C — Qualification market

Words such as “possible” and “attributed” are sold because their removal raises an asset's confidence score.

### Event D — Citation detachment

A footnote becomes a free-standing link, then returns as independent evidence.

### Event E — Sentence merger

Halves of two incompatible testimonies combine into a coherent institutional statement.

### Event F — Punctuation auction

Question marks and quotation marks receive prices. Their purchasers can preserve uncertainty; their sellers gain access.

### Event G — Administrative composting

Burned documents return as terms in a report's statistical summary.

### Event H — Audio cut

A clipped syllable from the artist's voice is rearranged into several possible words without declaring any reconstruction correct.

---

## 19. Visual behavior of contagion

Textual movement should be material and traceable:

- a word detaches from a paragraph;
- whitespace retains its dimensions;
- the word crosses a visible relation line;
- it enters a button, label or document;
- its typography gradually conforms to the host;
- a small mark indicates prior custody;
- inspection reveals its route;
- later laundering removes the mark.

Avoid liquid glitch effects that obscure the grammatical operation.

The reader should be able to say what moved and what changed because it moved.

---

## 20. Sound behavior of contagion

A mobile phrase carries a motif.

- witness host: unstable timing, breath, room noise;
- investigative host: bass articulation organizes the motif;
- archival host: repeated pulse and fixed duration;
- market host: compressed envelope, clear downbeat;
- canonical host: orchestral doubling makes the motif sound inevitable;
- visitor host: route rhythm places the motif inside the next loop.

Musical laundering cleans source noise while altering one interval. What sounds more authoritative is less faithful.

---

## 21. Hyperstitional loops by edition

Each edition selects one principal claim whose materialization is most advanced.

Possible edition claims:

- the blue photograph depicts the work;
- Elias Orr was a real collector;
- the artist intended failure as behavior;
- Version D preserves Version A;
- the 1998 exhibition occurred;
- the circular sale represented independent demand;
- the investigator authored the notes;
- the visitor's route is independent scholarship.

The claim begins at a different H-stage according to edition. The visitor's actions can move it forward or preserve friction.

No edition selects a canonical truth. It selects the fiction closest to becoming infrastructure.

---

## 22. Resistant language

Not every sentence should be equally susceptible to circulation.

Some passages resist because they are:

- too long to quote without distortion;
- attached to a gift;
- protected by the visitor;
- grammatically dependent on a named speaker;
- materially embedded in audio or layout;
- unwilling to resolve their pronouns;
- written in a form TRACE cannot classify.

The artist's rare passages should be especially difficult to convert into metadata.

Example:

> You can have the file. The part I mean is what happens when having it fails.

The system may price the sentence but cannot produce a clean asset from it without obvious damage.

---

## 23. Anti-hyperstition

The visitor cannot simply stop circulation, but can keep some claims from hardening.

Possible acts:

- retain the question mark;
- preserve a full witness statement;
- disclose dependence between sources;
- refuse TRACE registration;
- carry two originals without choosing;
- return a quotation to its context;
- mark a route as inherited rather than independent;
- decline the grammar of confirmation.

These acts preserve difficulty. They may reduce access, price and institutional credibility.

> Resistance is not silence. It is the preservation of expensive grammar.

---

## 24. Delusion and pattern

The work may generate a degree of madness through proliferating connections, but it must distinguish three kinds of relation:

1. **documented:** a traceable material connection exists;
2. **inferred:** the investigator proposes a connection;
3. **generated:** the interface placed two things together and the reader supplied causality.

The reader may confuse them. The system must not.

Paranoia becomes conceptually useful when the reader discovers that some patterns are real, some profitable and some consequences of the investigative instrument.

---

## 25. The future edits the past

Later loops can alter how earlier text is displayed without claiming to alter a blockchain.

Examples:

- an early uncertainty appears with a later citation count;
- a sentence previously unattributed now bears the visitor's role;
- a button chosen in loop one appears as a verb in a historical report;
- the opening commission cites the conclusion of a prior loop;
- a work title changes to accommodate what was reconstructed;
- the investigator's biography gains the journey the visitor completed.

The underlying authored corpus remains fixed. Its provenance state changes.

---

## 26. Rare event: the successful fiction

In some routes, one hypothesis reaches H7 — Canonical.

The interface changes:

- price disappears;
- qualification disappears;
- dissent moves into specialist access;
- the claim becomes part of navigation labels;
- new pages presuppose it;
- music resolves around its motif;
- the final report no longer lists it among findings.

The claim has become too foundational to appear as a claim.

This is not victory. It is the most complete form of disappearance.

---

## 27. Rare event: the failed fiction

A claim can lose circulation without being disproved.

Its fate:

- no current price;
- sources intact but unopened;
- no institutional sponsor;
- no compatible interface action;
- preserved only in a witness's long sentence;
- musically present as an unresolved figure.

The failed fiction may retain more truth than the successful one, but lacks the machinery to become historical.

---

## 28. The final report as infection vector

The report does not close the case. It packages the investigation for later citation.

Route-dependent language enters:

- the catalog;
- TRACE;
- the Continuity Office statement;
- the Market listing;
- the second appointment;
- the investigator's biography;
- the shared URL.

The final report is the most efficient host because it appears to summarize rather than intervene.

---

## 29. Corpus requirements

For each major claim, the corpus should eventually include:

- one uncertain human formulation;
- one careful investigative formulation;
- one archival classification;
- one market compression;
- one interface imperative;
- one historical retrospective;
- one damaged or resistant version;
- one version attributed to the visitor.

This creates authored mutation paths instead of algorithmic paraphrase.

---

## 30. Acceptance tests

The protocol succeeds if:

- claims create material consequences that later support them;
- the reader can trace at least one sentence across several hosts;
- cut-up operations preserve semantic causality;
- words retain custody histories;
- the investigator's personhood emerges from documentation;
- the visitor's personhood emerges from actions;
- TRACE records relations without becoming an oracle;
- canonical claims disappear into ordinary interface language;
- resistant sentences remain possible;
- paranoia arises from distinguishable classes of connection;
- no random collage substitutes for authored literature;
- later loops make earlier language appear retrospectively different.

---

## 31. Final rules

> Every cut transfers power.

> Every repetition changes custody.

> Every citation is an action performed upon the cited sentence.

> A fiction becomes historical not when everyone believes it, but when enough systems require it to have been true.
