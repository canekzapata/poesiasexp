# DRAMATURGY OF THE LOOP

## How an assistant becomes evidence

**Status:** governing temporal document · version 0.1  
**Finished-work language:** English  
**Primary arc:** commission → inquiry → market → complicity → report → reacquisition

---

## 1. Dramatic proposition

The visitor does not solve a crime. The visitor undergoes a change of legal, grammatical and historical position.

At entry:

> You are outside the record and have been asked to inspect it.

At exit:

> Your inspection is inside the record and has been acquired by the institution you inspected.

The loop is therefore not repetition. It is **reclassification**.

---

## 2. What must change

Across one complete reading, five transformations occur:

1. **reader → assistant** — navigation becomes assigned work;
2. **assistant → investigator** — choices begin carrying authority;
3. **investigator → trader** — knowledge requires exchange;
4. **trader → participant** — transactions alter the evidence;
5. **participant → evidence** — the route is cataloged and cited.

The text should register each change before the interface names it explicitly.

---

## 3. Duration

A first meaningful loop should last approximately 25–45 minutes for a patient reader.

A visitor can leave earlier and still encounter a shaped fragment. Completion is not required to experience the work.

The 256 chambers are the edition's possible field, not the expected length of a session. A normal first loop visits approximately:

- 18–32 chambers;
- 6–8 evidence objects;
- 3–5 irreversible exchanges;
- 2 competing hypotheses;
- 1 information run;
- 1 administrative conclusion.

Subsequent loops may be shorter because earlier actions return as citations and compressed residues.

---

## 4. Dramatic rhythm

The arc alternates concentration and disturbance:

```text
READ → NOTICE → CHOOSE → LOSE → REASSESS
          ↘          ↗
           LISTEN / WAIT
```

No more than two high-pressure chambers should appear consecutively. After proliferation, the reader needs a clearing in which the consequences become legible.

The work is not a thriller paced by constant escalation. It is an inquiry whose instruments gradually reveal their appetite.

---

# ACT I — APPOINTMENT

## 5. Position: outside the case

The visitor sees a restrained field resembling a research interface, not a game lobby or crypto dashboard.

The first screen contains:

- the commission letter;
- the work title;
- a small case header;
- one dominant action: `ACCEPT APPOINTMENT`;
- one less obvious action: `INSPECT CURRENT OWNER`;
- the possibility of refusing.

Sound:

- incomplete bass figure;
- dry room tone;
- no drums until the first consequential action;
- sufficient silence to read the letter without pressure.

### Dramatic knowledge

The visitor understands:

- there is a disputed work;
- a valid signature exists;
- provenance is under review;
- the commissioning institution owns the object of inquiry.

The visitor does not yet understand:

- that entering creates custody;
- that their route will be preserved;
- that the investigator may not exist;
- that every possible finding can benefit the Continuity Office.

---

## 6. Refusal

Refusal must be a real route, not a false button that immediately accepts on the visitor's behalf.

If the visitor declines:

> Appointment declined.  
> Independent visitor status recorded.

They may still inspect public materials, but:

- possess less authority;
- encounter more redaction;
- retain greater independence;
- accumulate fewer liabilities;
- receive different invitations from the Market;
- later discover that refusal itself has been cited as evidence of procedural openness.

Refusal does not keep the reader outside. It changes the manner of incorporation.

---

## 7. First irreversible verb

The first irreversible action should be small:

> VERIFY SIGNATURE

The result is exact but narrower than expected. The reader learns the piece's fundamental syntax:

1. an action promises knowledge;
2. the action produces a precise result;
3. the result exposes another category the system cannot address;
4. something peripheral becomes collateral.

The first drum event occurs here: a single settled pulse.

---

# ACT II — THE APPARENT CASE

## 8. Position: assistant

The investigator supplies calm directions. The visitor believes the task is to compare evidence.

Primary objects:

- valid signature;
- anticipatory photograph;
- mutilated catalog;
- first collector.

Interface verbs remain professional:

> COMPARE  
> VERIFY  
> REQUEST SOURCE  
> OPEN VERSO  
> LOCATE OWNER

The first links feel useful rather than culpable.

---

## 9. The first contradiction

The visitor should discover a contradiction through their own comparison:

- the photograph is dated 1998;
- the visible feature is attributed to software released later;
- the catalog page that might resolve the issue is missing.

No alarm announces the contradiction. Typography, proximity and perhaps a small musical dislocation allow the reader to formulate it.

Only after the visitor follows the relation does the interface state:

> RELATION ADDED TO CASE

This is the first indication that discovery changes the archive.

---

## 10. The beautiful clearing

After technical comparison comes a long witness passage about the artist's room.

The interface quiets:

- no visible prices;
- no proliferating buttons;
- bass thins to a slow figure;
- orchestral color appears for the first time;
- the passage cannot be interrupted for several seconds, though it can be exited accessibly.

The reader should care about the lost human context before being invited to trade it.

---

## 11. First hypothesis gate

The interface asks the visitor to preserve two of four provisional descriptions:

> EARLY WORK, POORLY ARCHIVED  
> VALID SIGNATURE, FALSE ATTRIBUTION  
> LATER FILE, LEGITIMATE RESTORATION  
> RETROSPECTIVE CONSTRUCTION

This is not a final accusation. Preserved hypotheses affect which evidence becomes cheaper, visible or suspicious.

The two unpreserved hypotheses do not disappear completely. They become expensive dissent.

---

# ACT III — THE PRICE OF CLARITY

## 12. Position: investigator

The visitor now receives a visible portfolio and enough credibility to perform classifications.

The language of buttons changes:

> ESTABLISH DATE  
> ACCEPT ATTRIBUTION  
> DESIGNATE SOURCE  
> CLASSIFY DERIVATIVE

The reader's actions are no longer merely observational.

---

## 13. First purchase

The photograph offers a precise date in exchange for its caption.

Before:

> Thursday — take the blue down before M. arrives  
> 1998?

Offer:

> PURCHASE DATE  
> Retain: 1998  
> Surrender: handwriting, question mark, relation to Thursday and M.

After:

> DATE: 1998

The date becomes easier to cite and less evidentially honest.

This transaction should feel useful. Regret may arrive later.

---

## 14. The first receipt

The receipt appears modestly in the margin. Later documents quote the clean date without showing the sacrifice.

When the visitor encounters the museum label:

> Created 1998.

a small relation may reveal:

> supported by independent dating operation

The operation is the visitor's purchase.

This is the first moment of complicity that can be recognized retrospectively.

---

## 15. Authority arrives

The investigator begins using “we”:

> We can now establish that the date entered the record before the present review concluded.

The pronoun quietly includes the visitor.

The visitor's portfolio is renamed:

> ASSISTANT MATERIALS

becomes:

> INVESTIGATIVE HOLDINGS

No announcement marks the promotion.

---

# ACT IV — THE MARKET OF EVIDENCE

## 16. Position: trader

The Market becomes visible only after the visitor possesses something it can price.

It does not introduce itself as a separate world. Prices appear beside existing claims. Footnotes gain offers. Buttons acquire balances.

Examples:

> WITNESS MEMORY — 18 CONTEXT  
> SIGNER LABEL — MARKET DEPTH LOW  
> MISSING PAGE — OFFERS ACTIVE  
> YOUR DATE — CITED 3 TIMES

The bass begins moving independently. Drums establish forward momentum.

---

## 17. The first tempting sale

The intimate witness statement occupies a custody slot and can be sold for enough context to open the circular sale.

Offer:

> SELL TESTIMONY  
> Receive: 9 context + 1 custody  
> Retain: source, price and institutional summary  
> Lose: wording, pauses and material detail

If sold, the passage becomes:

> Former collaborator confirms blue-screen behavior in the artist's studio.

If retained, another route closes temporarily.

Neither choice is morally pure. Retention is also possession.

---

## 18. The market's first theorem

> Price does not prove importance. It produces a costly public situation in which many qualified people are invited to explain why the price was reasonable.

After reading it, the visitor sees that their own evidence has acquired estimated value.

The case is no longer simply about the historical sale. It is reproducing the sale's logic in miniature.

---

## 19. The collector branch

Investigating Elias Orr produces nested addresses, registrations and citations. Buttons multiply, but each belongs to a discernible chain.

The visitor eventually must accept one biographical statement to continue. Once accepted, the statement appears in two sources.

This is the first overt demonstration that interface compliance creates apparent corroboration.

---

# ACT V — PROLIFERATION

## 20. Position: overextended investigator

The case appears most solvable immediately before it becomes unmanageable.

Triggers may include:

- opening a threshold number of relations;
- carrying incompatible originals;
- acquiring stolen evidence;
- accumulating liability;
- following the collector through multiple jurisdictions;
- reaching a high documentary-density score.

Proliferation must feel caused by the visitor's method.

---

## 21. Button multiplication

Buttons generate descendants by semantic consequence:

```text
LOCATE OWNER
└─ LOCATE ADDRESS
   ├─ LOCATE REGISTERED AGENT
   │  ├─ PURCHASE JURISDICTION
   │  └─ VERIFY DISSOLUTION
   └─ INSPECT CURRENT OCCUPANT
      └─ ACCEPT COOKIES TO CONTINUE SEARCHING FOR A PERSON
```

Rules:

- every new button descends from a prior verb;
- no button appears merely as visual noise;
- labels remain readable;
- proliferation has a maximum density;
- keyboard focus follows a stable order;
- reduced-motion mode preserves conceptual multiplication without spatial chaos.

---

## 22. The information run

The Market reports instability:

> CONFIDENCE REMAINS HIGH.  
> ACCESS TO SUPPORTING CONTEXT MAY BE INTERRUPTED.

Offers proliferate:

> PRESERVE QUOTATION  
> SELL BEFORE CORRECTION  
> ABANDON SOURCE  
> CONVERT MEMORY  
> EXIT WITH ATTRIBUTION

Text migrates from paragraphs into buttons. A verb is physically removed from a sentence and appears as an instrument of action.

The music accelerates, but the reader is never subject to a countdown. Apparent urgency and actual time remain distinct.

---

## 23. The theft

A button steals a meaningful word from a document.

Before:

> The Archive preserved the uncertainty by assigning it a permanent identifier.

After:

> The Archive ________ the uncertainty by assigning it a permanent identifier.

Button:

> PRESERVED — ACQUIRED

The stolen verb becomes part of a later action:

> PRESERVE CURRENT ATTRIBUTION

If used, it may enter the final report as something the visitor did.

---

## 24. Clearing after the run

Proliferation ends abruptly in a nearly empty chamber.

Visible:

- receipts;
- missing-word spaces;
- one intact sentence;
- the visitor's current liability;
- perhaps the cut voice note.

Sound:

- drums stop;
- bass retains a pulse but no pitch sequence;
- orchestral residue holds a chord missing its inner voice;
- optional complete silence.

The visitor has time to understand what the activity removed.

---

# ACT VI — THE INVESTIGATOR UNDER REVIEW

## 25. Position: participant

Evidence about the investigator begins appearing among evidence about the artwork.

Signals:

- an expense report bears the historical wallet's signature;
- the investigator appears on the exhibition attendance list;
- their credential was issued after their first cited note;
- handwriting attributed to them appears in incompatible forms;
- the appointment letter cites preliminary findings not yet written.

The visitor initially treats these as a secondary mystery. The interface treats them as continuation of the same provenance problem.

---

## 26. Grammatical reassignment

The investigator's “I” becomes unstable.

Earlier:

> I compared the two versions.

Later:

> We compared the two versions.

Then:

> The investigator compared the two versions.

Finally:

> Two versions were compared.

The visitor may recover the missing agent only by accepting attribution.

---

## 27. The visitor's statement

The interface assembles a first-person statement from actions already taken:

> I verified the artist's signature, dated the earliest image, established the original file and excluded a conflicting account before consulting the market record.

This statement is locally accurate and globally misleading.

Options:

> SIGN STATEMENT  
> CORRECT STATEMENT — COST: 31 CONTEXT  
> ATTRIBUTE TO INVESTIGATOR  
> CONTINUE WITHOUT AUTHOR

Every choice creates a different form of liability.

---

## 28. Recognition scene

The crucial recognition is not a surprise confession. It is an exact structural realization:

> The visitor has performed upon the evidence the same operations being investigated: attribution, substitution, pricing, removal of conflict and retrospective construction.

The work should not accuse them theatrically. It should show the ledger.

---

# ACT VII — REPORT

## 29. Position: evidence producer

The interface becomes calm again. The visual field resembles the opening, but now contains:

- visitor-generated findings;
- receipts;
- abandoned materials;
- hypothesis weights;
- liability;
- the route identifier.

The report is assembled from the surviving language. Missing context changes its certainty.

---

## 30. Variable findings

Every final report contains:

### Invariant conclusion

> No single act was identified by which a false work entered an otherwise true history.

### Route-dependent paragraph

This paragraph reflects:

- which original was designated;
- which witness was preserved;
- whether the first collector was accepted as a person;
- whether the circular sale was inspected;
- which source was burned;
- how much liability accumulated.

### Administrative classification

```text
INVALID SIGNATURE: NOT OBSERVED
UNAUTHORIZED TRANSFER: NOT OBSERVED
MEDIA CONTINUITY: OUTSIDE SCOPE
HUMAN IDENTITY: OUTSIDE SCOPE
RETROSPECTIVE MANUFACTURE OF ORIGIN: CATEGORY NOT FOUND
```

### Institutional extraction

The Continuity Office summarizes the report more favorably than the report summarizes itself.

---

## 31. No solution reward

There is no “correct culprit,” completion badge or percentage of truth discovered.

Instead, the final screen may show:

- evidence retained;
- context destroyed;
- claims created;
- sources abandoned;
- liability inherited;
- number of future citations generated;
- the visitor's route classification.

The reader receives an account of their method.

---

## 32. The minority sentence

If sufficient uncertainty survived, the investigator can insert:

> The absence of a category is not the absence of an event.

If it did not survive, the sentence appears only as:

> CATEGORY NOT FOUND.

The economy determines not only access to evidence but the final report's capacity for thought.

---

# ACT VIII — ACQUISITION AND RETURN

## 33. Position: evidence

After the report, the Continuity Office announces:

> The investigator's archive has been acquired to ensure continuing access for qualified scholarship.

The visitor's route receives:

- a catalog number;
- a title;
- a provenance line;
- an estimated value;
- a list of notable actions;
- a status within TRACE.

Their navigation has become the next disputed work.

---

## 34. The transition

The page does not reset suddenly.

Possible sequence:

1. the report is compressed into a catalog entry;
2. its paragraphs collapse into metadata fields;
3. metadata fields settle into the opening grid;
4. the case title changes;
5. the opening bass motif returns using the visitor's route rhythm;
6. the appointment letter addresses the same browser under a new case number.

The transformation should take long enough to be understood and short enough to preserve inevitability.

---

## 35. Second appointment

First loop:

> Review the provenance of *A Form of Remaining Outside*.

Second loop:

> Review the provenance of *Inquiry into A Form of Remaining Outside*.

The disputed artwork may now be the investigator's report. The original work becomes supporting material.

The reader understands that historical authority can migrate from object to investigation.

---

## 36. What the loop remembers

The second loop can retain:

- one phrase protected by the visitor;
- one missing word;
- the route's principal rhythm;
- accumulated liability;
- the chosen original;
- the first hypothesis abandoned;
- a receipt for the most intimate evidence sold;
- the visitor's assigned role;
- the previous report's institutional summary.

It should not retain every detail. Forgetting is part of the structure.

---

## 37. What changes on later loops

### Loop 2 — Provenance of the inquiry

The visitor investigates the investigator. Earlier choices appear as historical evidence.

### Loop 3 — Provenance of the visitor

The system questions who inherited, copied or authored the route. Pronouns and custody become central.

### Loop 4+ — Compression

The work becomes shorter and more administrative. Rich language has been converted into records, prices and references. Rare passages may resist this compression.

The loops should not imply endless content. They reveal repeated institutional operations applied to progressively poorer material.

---

## 38. Escape conditions

The loop has no triumphant escape, but several meaningful refusals:

- close the tab, allowing unpreserved language to disappear;
- return a gifted testimony rather than selling it;
- refuse to establish an original;
- preserve contradictory evidence until the report cannot conclude;
- decline the second appointment;
- share a route labeled as inherited rather than independent;
- remain in the empty chamber and listen.

These do not defeat the system. They preserve specific relations the system prefers to liquidate.

---

## 39. Leaving the tab

Closing the tab should matter conceptually but must not involve deceptive claims.

Ephemeral state may disappear unless encoded in the URL. Before exit, the work can truthfully state:

> Unsaved context exists only in this browser session.  
> Closing the tab will not destroy the record.  
> It will destroy your present relation to it.

No fake threat of blockchain loss. No dark pattern preventing exit.

---

## 40. Dramatic use of browser history

The back button is part of the work.

Possible behaviors:

- returns to a prior chamber whose evidence state has changed;
- restores layout but not surrendered language;
- reveals that a prior title has been corrected;
- shows receipts where full documents once appeared;
- allows comparison between navigational memory and current custody.

History must remain navigable and must never trap the visitor.

---

## 41. Dramatic use of refresh

Refresh reconstructs the route from the URL.

The page may distinguish:

> SESSION MEMORY RESTORED  
> READING TIME NOT RECOVERABLE  
> UNSAVED ATTENTION LOST

Refresh demonstrates that reproducible state is not reproducible experience.

---

## 42. Dramatic use of multiple tabs

Opening a second tab creates a second custody claim over the same route.

Without server synchronization, the work can still compare local tabs through browser facilities when available, with graceful fallback.

Possible event:

> This evidence is currently open under another reading.  
> Simultaneous access has been classified as shared custody.

Multiple tabs should enrich the concept, never be required.

---

## 43. Dramatic failures

### Too much clarity

If the visitor rapidly selects one hypothesis, the system produces a clean report and exposes its dependence on discarded alternatives.

### Too much skepticism

If the visitor refuses every attribution, they preserve uncertainty but lose the ability to make certain relations visible.

### Too much accumulation

If the visitor hoards evidence, custody pressure forces displacement and the collection begins resembling the institution it opposes.

### Too much trading

If the visitor liquidates context, the report becomes confident, profitable and semantically thin.

No play style is declared correct. Each reveals a different institutional appetite.

---

## 44. Pacing safeguards

- Never place a transaction before the reader has encountered something worth preserving.
- Never make all doors costly at once.
- Never place dense music beneath the densest prose.
- Never require speed to protect evidence.
- Never introduce the investigator's ontological doubt before the original case feels substantial.
- Never turn the visitor into a culprit through an undisclosed real-world data action.
- Never explain the complete loop before the first transaction.
- Always provide a calm chamber after proliferation.
- Let important passages remain readable without interaction.

---

## 45. Prototype arc

A first prototype can test the entire dramaturgy in sixteen chambers:

1. appointment;
2. signature;
3. photograph;
4. witness clearing;
5. date purchase;
6. catalog gap;
7. hypothesis gate;
8. first collector;
9. evidence market;
10. testimony sale or retention;
11. information run;
12. empty aftermath;
13. investigator anomaly;
14. assembled visitor statement;
15. report;
16. second appointment.

If this sequence does not produce the conceptual transformation, expanding to 256 chambers will not repair it.

---

## 46. Dramatic acceptance tests

The loop succeeds if:

- the reader initially trusts the task more than the institution;
- the first transaction feels useful rather than obviously sinister;
- the witness material creates something worth losing;
- the Market appears as a transformation of existing evidence;
- proliferation has semantic ancestry;
- the visitor understands how their actions produced claims;
- the investigator mystery deepens rather than replaces the artwork mystery;
- the final report reflects actual route decisions;
- the return changes the status of the reader;
- a second loop is conceptually different, not just shuffled;
- leaving remains possible and meaningful;
- textual comprehension remains the primary pleasure.

---

## 47. Final dramatic rule

Every phase must change at least one of these:

- what the visitor can know;
- what the visitor can say;
- what the visitor owns;
- what the visitor owes;
- what the visitor has become.

If a chamber changes only location, it is not part of the loop.

> The first investigation asks whether the work has a false past.  
> The second asks whether the investigator has any past outside the work.
