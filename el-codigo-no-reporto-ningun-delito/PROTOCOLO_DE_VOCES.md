# PROTOCOLO DE VOCES

## Who is speaking when a document speaks?

**Status:** governing literary document · version 0.1  
**Language of the finished work:** English

---

## 1. Canonical decision

The principal investigator is a forensic art historian commissioned twenty years after the disputed event by **The Continuity Office**, which currently holds the work.

The Continuity Office claims to seek clarity. Every clarification increases the documentary density, institutional visibility and possible value of its collection.

The visitor enters as the investigator's assistant. Their initial tasks appear neutral:

> Verify the signature.  
> Locate the technician.  
> Restore the missing page.  
> Establish first ownership.

Each verb already contains a theory of the case.

Gradually, the visitor discovers that nobody has communicated with the investigator directly. The investigator is known through:

- field notes;
- expense reports;
- credentials;
- search histories;
- institutional correspondence;
- quotations in later scholarship;
- a contractual signature;
- corrections made in several incompatible hands.

The investigator may be a person, a shared professional identity, an automated research process or a character manufactured to give the archive a discoverer.

The work never resolves this.

---

## 2. Central grammatical question

> Who acquires the right to say “I” when every speaker is known only through records?

The first person is not automatically authentic. The third person is not automatically distant. Institutional passive voice is not neutral. Metadata has grammatical agency even when it lacks intention.

Pronouns are positions in the provenance system:

- **I** — the current claimant to direct experience;
- **we** — an institution distributing responsibility;
- **you** — the visitor being recruited into an operation;
- **they** — an actor whose incentives remain unexamined;
- **it** — an object being denied agency or a system quietly exercising it;
- **no one** — a decision successfully dissolved into procedure.

## 3. The investigator

### Function

Connects evidence and supplies the apparent continuity of the inquiry.

### What the investigator can know

- documentary relations;
- technical distinctions;
- contradictions between accounts;
- changes in public language;
- the route already taken through the case.

### What the investigator cannot know

- who controlled the historical key;
- what the artist intended;
- what file occupied the link at the decisive instant;
- whether the commission itself is sincere;
- whether their own biography precedes the investigation.

### Desire

To give the case a shape that can survive publication.

### Hidden interest

An unresolved but structured case may be more valuable than a solved one.

### Register

Precise, comparative, patient. Long syntax when tracing relations; short syntax when reaching a limit.

### Typical verbs

compare, establish, distinguish, attribute, recover, qualify, observe, exclude.

### Forbidden certainty

The investigator never writes “this proves the work is authentic” or “this proves fraud.” Conclusions must name exactly what has been established.

### Sample

> I was retained to determine whether the provenance had been manufactured. The contract did not specify whether discovering a manufacture would include my own part in it.

### Corruption arc

The investigator begins by annotating documents. Later documents annotate the investigator. Eventually their assertions appear without quotation marks inside institutional prose.

---

## 4. The assistant / visitor

### Function

Performs the verbs that the investigator can no longer perform without compromising the inquiry.

### What the visitor can know

Only what remains available along their edition and route.

### Desire

Initially: access. Later: coherence, acquittal or possession.

### Hidden interest

The visitor wants their particular route to matter.

### Register

The visitor does not begin with authored prose. Their voice is assembled from choices, accepted statements, discarded words and interface actions.

### Transformation

Commands become biographical statements:

> OPEN THE CATALOG  
> You opened the catalog.  
> The catalog was opened under your authority.  
> A researcher authorized the opening.  
> Early research established access to the catalog.

By the end, the system can quote the visitor without the visitor ever having typed a sentence.

---

## 5. The Continuity Office

### Function

Commissions the investigation and owns the disputed work.

### Temporal anomaly

Its legal existence begins after the exhibition it claims to have supported.

### Desire

To convert uncertainty into a governable asset.

### Register

Elegant institutional English: plural pronouns, calibrated concern, reputational warmth, no visible author.

### Typical phrases

- in the interest of transparency;
- based on currently available materials;
- without prejudice to future findings;
- our ongoing commitment to responsible stewardship;
- the historical record continues to evolve.

### Grammatical defense

Uses **we** for values and passive voice for actions.

> We welcome further scholarship. The relevant file appears to have been replaced.

### Comic pressure

Its transparency statements progressively obscure who commissioned, funded and approved them.

---

## 6. The artist

### Function

The absent center whose life is being converted into credentials.

### Rule

The artist must never become a mystical oracle who foresaw the blockchain. She remains materially situated, funny, inconsistent and sometimes bored by questions of authorship.

### Available materials

- a cut voice note;
- folder names;
- brief emails;
- comments in code;
- recollections by others;
- refusals to distinguish tests from finished work.

### Register

Concrete, impatient, attentive to color, behavior and display. Less interested in ownership than everyone who quotes her.

### Sample

> It isn't the blue file. It's what the blue does when the machine fails to find the other part.

### Prohibition

No recovered manifesto should solve the conceptual work for us.

---

## 7. The wallet

### Function

Makes valid statements about action and invalid suggestions about identity.

### Knowledge

Transactions, signatures, balances, permissions.

### Inability

Motive, biography, coercion, custody outside the key.

### Register

First-person minimalism composed of performed operations.

> I signed.  
> I did not say who held me.

### Danger

Other voices quote the wallet's **I** as though it belonged to the artist.

---

## 8. The contract

### Function

Executes categories designed by others.

### Register

Conditional, literal, indifferent, occasionally comic through precision.

> The transfer satisfied the transfer conditions. Authenticity was not among the transfer conditions.

### Knowledge

State, permission, successful execution.

### Inability

Crime, care, fraud, grief, artistic identity.

### Rule

The contract is not a robot philosopher. Its conceptual force comes from refusing every category it was not written to evaluate.

---

## 9. The archive

### Function

Selects what can later appear to have survived.

### Desire

Continuity, institutional relevance and fundable preservation.

### Register

Custodial, taxonomic and regretful.

> No original file was located. For preservation purposes, the earliest available derivative has been designated the source.

### Blind spot

Treats naming as conservation and conservation as evidence of prior existence.

### Voice mutation

When evidence is missing, the archive speaks more fully, not less.

---

## 10. The market

### Function

Writes causes backward from price.

### Register

Compressed confidence: superlatives, milestones, scarcity, newly discovered inevitability.

> Long considered inaccessible, the work now emerges as a defining early statement.

### Knowledge

Offers, attention, liquidity, comparables.

### Inability

Importance except as predicted demand.

### Grammatical behavior

Removes agents from valuation:

> Interest grew.  
> A market emerged.  
> The work achieved recognition.

### Musical relation

Market statements increase bass pressure while cooling harmonic color.

---

## 11. The historian

### Function

Speaks from a later time in which provisional claims have become standard references.

### Register

Measured, elegant, footnoted, dangerous through compression.

> Although the circumstances of its first exhibition remain disputed, its presence there is now generally accepted.

### Blind spot

Treats repeated uncertainty as settled scholarly context.

### Relation to investigator

May be the investigator writing later, another person quoting them, or a voice generated from the archive's stabilized language.

---

## 12. The witness

### Function

Returns matter, fatigue, rooms and bodies to the case.

### Register

Sensory precision with chronological fragility.

> I remember carrying the monitor because the stairwell was too narrow for both of us. I don't remember what the monitor showed. I remember that she laughed when it went blue.

### Knowledge

What an event felt like.

### Inability

How that feeling maps onto filenames, dates and titles.

### Ethical rule

Witnesses are not quaint unreliable narrators. Their uncertainty may be more honest than the archive's precision.

---

## 13. The absent technician

### Function

Appears to be the one person capable of resolving the material question.

### Presence

Only through failed deliveries, inconsistent names, invoices and other people's references.

### Register

The technician has no stable direct voice. Attempts to contact them generate administrative language.

> Recipient unknown.  
> The address exists.  
> The addressee could not be established.

### Rule

Each attempt to locate the technician produces more infrastructure and less person.

---

## 14. The interface

### Function

Distributes permission while presenting itself as neutral navigation.

### Register

Imperative, concise, operational.

> VERIFY  
> ACQUIRE CONTEXT  
> ACCEPT ATTRIBUTION  
> REMOVE CONFLICT  
> CONTINUE WITHOUT WITNESS

### Corruption

Buttons begin as tools and end as narrators. A button can steal a verb from a paragraph, inherit its object and later appear in a report as an action already taken.

### Rule

Every interface verb must state a real literary consequence. Avoid generic `NEXT`, `MORE` and `SUBMIT` when a more culpable verb exists.

---

## 15. The link

### Function

Claims that two things belong in sequence.

### Register

Invitation at first, assertion later.

> See related material.  
> This material is related.  
> The relationship is documented.  
> The documented relationship is historically significant.

### Conceptual role

A hyperlink is the smallest unit of fabricated provenance: an actionable claim that one object should be understood through another.

---

## 16. Voice laundering

A sentence can migrate through the system:

### Stage 1 — Memory

> I think the screen went blue when the cable came loose.

### Stage 2 — Interview note

> Witness recalls a blue screen associated with equipment failure.

### Stage 3 — Conservation record

> Blue-screen behavior documented in early display conditions.

### Stage 4 — Catalog

> The work's characteristic blue failure-state was present in its earliest installation.

### Stage 5 — Market

> Featuring the iconic blue failure-state seen in the original installation.

Every stage is locally plausible. Together they manufacture certainty.

The interface should sometimes let the visitor observe, accelerate or interrupt this process.

---

## 17. Attribution display

Not every voice should be labeled immediately.

Possible states:

- named source;
- institutional source;
- source purchasable;
- source redacted;
- source inferred by typography or sound;
- source revealed only after the passage is quoted elsewhere;
- source transferred to the visitor;
- source lost while the sentence survives.

The reader must always be able to distinguish intentional uncertainty from accidental UI confusion.

---

## 18. Distribution across a chamber

A normal chamber contains no more than three active voices:

1. a dominant voice presenting a claim;
2. a countervoice introducing material resistance;
3. the interface requesting an action.

High-proliferation chambers may contain many quotations, but their collision must have a designed hierarchy.

Intimate witness passages should rarely be interrupted.

---

## 19. Style tests

For every authored fragment:

- Could another voice plausibly say this unchanged? If yes, differentiate it.
- Does the speaker know more than their position permits? If yes, revise or mark the leak as evidence.
- Is passive voice concealing an actor? Decide whether that concealment is deliberate.
- Does technical confidence exceed the underlying proof?
- What does the speaker gain if quoted?
- What remains after attribution disappears?
- Can the sentence be productively stolen by a button or another document?

---

## 20. Closing proposition

The two central mysteries mirror one another:

1. Does the work exist as the thing its documents describe?
2. Does the investigator exist as the person their documents describe?

The visitor cannot answer either without producing further documentation.

> If a fictional investigator produces valid evidence, does the evidence become fictional—or does the investigator become real?

---

## 21. Institutional naming

Institutional names remain short and operational:

- **The Continuity Office** preserves the claim of continuity;
- **The Registry** turns relations into records;
- **The Archive** determines what can count as surviving;
- **The Market** converts attention into retrospective importance.

Acronyms may appear when the abbreviation itself makes an argument. The Registry's principal system, **TRACE**—Transfer Record for Attribution, Continuity and Evidence—sounds like the thing it claims to preserve while recording only the order in which claims refer to one another.

An acronym must expose, compress or disguise a conceptual contradiction. Otherwise the institution keeps its plain name.
