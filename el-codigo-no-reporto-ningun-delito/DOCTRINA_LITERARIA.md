# DOCTRINA LITERARIA

## English as evidence, interface and crime scene

**Status:** governing document · version 0.1  
**Language of the finished work:** English  
**Language of production notes:** Spanish or English as useful

---

## 1. First principle

The work is literature before it is an interface.

No animation, musical event, wallet metaphor or generative system earns its place unless it changes the meaning, ownership, credibility or survival of a sentence.

The reader should not feel that prose has been inserted into a digital artwork. The browser itself should appear to have been captured by a novel.

> The text is not content delivered by the system.  
> The system is one of the conditions under which the text can be believed.

---

## 2. Literary constellation

The desired writing may draw on broad traditions rather than reproduce one author's recognizable style:

- encyclopedic and systems-oriented fiction;
- paranoid narratives in which connections may be discoveries or symptoms;
- documentary novels and false archives;
- historiographic metafiction;
- procedural and constrained writing;
- conceptual literature;
- legal, financial and bureaucratic language turned against itself;
- detective fiction whose investigation changes the crime;
- comic institutional voices;
- hypertext fiction and browser-native poetics.

The relevant Pynchonian qualities are structural:

- systems that behave like characters;
- technical knowledge with comic pressure;
- sudden shifts between the intimate and the planetary;
- conspiracies indistinguishable from incentives;
- names, acronyms and organizations that may be jokes but still wield power;
- entropy, recursion and failed communication;
- intellectual density interrupted by tenderness, embarrassment or song.

We will not imitate sentence patterns, signature diction, characters or passages from any specific novelist.

---

## 3. What “learned” means here

The prose should be informed, not merely decorated with terminology.

Technical material must perform reasoning. It should understand distinctions such as:

- a signature proves control of a key, not civil identity;
- a timestamp proves inclusion or ordering within a system, not the date of artistic creation;
- a token can remain stable while referenced media changes;
- provenance is a chain of claims, custody events and interpretations, not a single field;
- a transaction can be valid and economically circular;
- immutability can preserve ambiguity;
- archives produce authority through selection as well as conservation;
- market price can become evidence in later historical narratives;
- consensus determines state, not motive;
- availability, authenticity, integrity and attribution are separate questions.

Whenever jargon appears, at least one consequence must follow from its exact meaning.

---

## 4. The governing intelligence

The novel's intelligence should be capable of holding several explanations at once without becoming vague.

It is:

- suspicious but not credulous;
- technically precise but not technophilic;
- comic without treating loss as trivial;
- erudite without lecturing continuously;
- self-implicating rather than morally superior;
- lyrical at moments of material disappearance;
- cold when institutions describe human events;
- tender toward failed custodians, minor witnesses and obsolete devices.

The work does not announce that everything is unknowable. It demonstrates exactly what each system can and cannot know.

---

## 5. Narrative temperature

The text should move among four temperatures.

### Cold

Contracts, ledgers, forms, receipts, checksums, administrative conclusions.

> Signature verified. Signer undetermined.

### Clinical

The investigator compares files and states limited conclusions.

> The image predates the catalog scan. It does not necessarily predate the catalog.

### Feverish

Connections proliferate, buttons acquire verbs, subsidiaries and addresses recur, syntax lengthens.

> By the time the third custodian denied possessing the key, the key had acquired counsel, a mailing address in a jurisdiction composed chiefly of mailing addresses, and a preference for being described as legacy infrastructure.

### Intimate

A witness remembers light, fatigue, dust, a room, a person who could have answered.

> I remember the blue on the wall. I do not remember the work. This may be the same memory.

No regime should dominate for too long. Density needs clearings.

---

## 6. The comic principle

Comedy prevents the project from becoming solemn crypto noir.

Its sources include:

- companies founded to certify that another company once existed;
- a compliance form asking the absent artist to confirm her absence;
- a wallet described as declining to comment;
- a provenance insurer requiring proof that uncertainty is accidental;
- a DAO voting on whether a previous vote happened;
- an archive charging a preservation fee for a missing file;
- legal disclaimers that become longer than the evidence;
- acronyms whose expansions change across documents.

The joke must reveal a system, not merely decorate it.

---

## 7. Human gravity

The puzzle needs someone or something worth mourning.

The emotional center is not “a valuable NFT may be fake.” It is that a human life has been reduced to reusable credentials. The artist's old tools, unfinished work and relationships become infrastructure for claims she cannot answer.

Recurring intimate materials:

- a blue rectangle of light on a studio wall;
- a voice note cut off before a proper noun;
- dust on the back of a monitor;
- a folder named `later_final`;
- a collaborator who remembers carrying equipment but not what it displayed;
- the artist's refusal, while alive, to distinguish sketches from works;
- a mundane receipt whose date becomes historically dangerous.

Without this register, the mystery becomes clever but weightless.

---

## 8. Sentence architecture

Use contrast rather than uniform maximalism.

### Long sentences

For networks of implication, institutional nesting, financial circulation and paranoid accumulation. They must remain grammatically controlled.

### Short sentences

For verdicts, interfaces, absences and reversals.

> The hash matched.  
> The image did not.

### Fragments

For damaged evidence, sold language and incomplete testimony.

### Lists

For bureaucratic comedy, inventories and false completeness. A list should acquire pressure through ordering.

### Repetition

For semantic laundering. The same sentence changes meaning when spoken by a contract, historian, market and witness.

---

## 9. Polyphony

The work has no neutral omniscient narrator.

Every statement must have a source position, even when that source is temporarily hidden. Voices may quote one another incorrectly. Institutional prose may absorb a witness's sentence. A button may steal a verb and thereby become its speaker.

The same proposition can pass through several forms:

1. testimony: “I saw it there.”
2. report: “A witness recalls seeing the work.”
3. metadata: `EXHIBITION_HISTORY: 1`
4. listing: “Historically exhibited.”
5. scholarship: “Its early exhibition history is well established.”

The transformation is the crime in miniature.

---

## 10. Reader cognition

The reader should experience alternating pleasures:

- understanding a technically exact distinction;
- discovering a recurring phrase;
- suspecting a relationship;
- recognizing that the relationship may have been generated by the interface;
- making a consequential choice;
- losing a desired passage;
- laughing at an institution;
- encountering a sudden beautiful sentence;
- realizing that their route now functions as evidence.

Confusion is allowed only when structured. The reader may not know what happened, but should know what kind of uncertainty they face.

---

## 11. Information density

Each chamber should contain one dominant claim, one anomaly and one action.

It may contain deep secondary material, but the surface must remain readable. Complexity should arise across links and transformations, not from making every paragraph equally opaque.

Recommended rhythm:

1. legible proposition;
2. precise complication;
3. action or cost;
4. residue.

---

## 12. Documents inside the novel

The corpus may include:

- chain-analysis reports;
- catalog entries;
- conservation notes;
- deposition fragments;
- marketplace listings;
- wallet annotations;
- insurance exclusions;
- curatorial essays;
- invoices;
- image descriptions;
- dead-link notices;
- governance proposals;
- private letters;
- automated risk reports;
- corrections to previous corrections;
- the investigator's field notebook.

Each genre requires its own rhetoric and blind spots.

---

## 13. Rules for English

- Write in precise international English.
- Allow technical, legal and art-historical registers to collide.
- Avoid generic “mysterious” diction.
- Avoid cyberpunk clichés, hacker spectacle and mystical claims about code.
- Prefer concrete operations over abstract declarations.
- Let ambiguity arise from evidence, grammar and incentives.
- Use rare vocabulary only when it is the exact word.
- Name institutions by the operation they perform. Prefer short conceptual names such as **The Continuity Office**, **The Registry**, **The Archive** and **The Market**. Acronyms are welcome when they perform conceptual work—when their abbreviation becomes a claim, conceals a contradiction or changes meaning across documents. Avoid acronyms used only as ornamental eccentricity.
- Preserve moments of radical simplicity.

---

## 14. Generative writing constraints

The hash may select, reorder and transform authored material. It should not synthesize arbitrary prose word by word.

The authored corpus supplies:

- sentence modules with controlled referents;
- variant descriptions of the same evidence;
- omissions and substitutions;
- transitions between voices;
- phrases capable of migration;
- lists whose elements can be reordered meaningfully;
- long-form passages reserved for particular regimes.

Generation governs composition, access and mutation. Authorship governs sentences.

---

## 15. Tests for every passage

Before inclusion, ask:

1. Who is speaking?
2. What can this speaker know?
3. What do they gain if believed?
4. Which exact distinction does the passage make?
5. What changes if the reader acts on it?
6. Is there a more concrete image or operation?
7. Does the passage remain interesting after removing blockchain vocabulary?
8. Could it have appeared in any crypto artwork? If yes, rewrite it.

---

## 16. Working thesis

> Provenance is literature with enforcement mechanisms.

The novel investigates how a chain of sentences becomes a chain of title, how a chain of title becomes a price, and how a price recruits historians to write its causes backward.
