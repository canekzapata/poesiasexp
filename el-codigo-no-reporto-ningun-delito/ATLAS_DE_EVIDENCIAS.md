# ATLAS DE EVIDENCIAS

## Twelve objects that cannot agree on what happened

**Status:** conceptual inventory · version 0.1  
**Finished-work language:** English

---

## Governing rule

Every piece of evidence has four layers:

1. what it materially is;
2. what it claims;
3. what the market says it proves;
4. what happens to it when the visitor investigates it.

No evidence is merely exposition. Every object can be bought, damaged, reclassified, quoted, emptied or made more authoritative by attention.

---

## E01 — The valid signature

**Object:** a signed message from the historical wallet.  
**Claim:** the wallet recognizes the work's identifier.  
**Anomaly:** the message contains no title, image hash or unambiguous date.  
**Favors:** legitimate attribution; recontextualized signature.  
**Cost to inspect:** surrender the visible name of the presumed signer.  
**Transformation:** the signature verifies as the identity label disappears.  
**Residue:** `SIGNATURE VALID / SIGNER WITHHELD`.

Sample:

> The signature answers the only question cryptography was asked. The archive had asked another and filed the answer under the same number.

---

## E02 — The anticipatory photograph

**Object:** a physical photograph showing a blue rectangular form in a studio or exhibition room.  
**Claim:** the disputed work was shown years before minting.  
**Anomaly:** one visible motif appears to depend on software released later.  
**Favors:** early exhibition; later substitution; retrospective resemblance.  
**Cost to inspect:** trade away the photograph's caption.  
**Transformation:** enlarging detail destroys chronological context.  
**Residue:** an image with no date, or a date with no image.

---

## E03 — The mutilated catalog

**Object:** an exhibition catalog missing pages 31–32.  
**Claim:** its index implies that a work of the correct dimensions occupied page 31.  
**Anomaly:** dimensions were added in different ink.  
**Favors:** undocumented exhibition; later catalog intervention.  
**Cost to inspect:** one previously visited chamber becomes unreachable.  
**Transformation:** the visitor can restore either title or dimensions, never both.  
**Residue:** two staples and an index entry.

---

## E04 — The first collector

**Object:** a dossier containing biography, address, purchases and quotations.  
**Claim:** an early collector acquired the work directly.  
**Anomaly:** every independent-looking source ultimately cites a marketplace press release.  
**Favors:** private collector; pseudonym; manufactured buyer.  
**Cost to inspect:** the visitor must accept one unsupported biographical fact.  
**Transformation:** accepted facts proliferate into profile buttons.  
**Residue:** a person-shaped network of citations.

---

## E05 — The stable token / unstable file

**Object:** an immutable token reference pointing toward mutable or incompletely preserved media.  
**Claim:** the token has always represented the same work.  
**Anomaly:** witnesses possess incompatible screenshots with plausible timestamps.  
**Favors:** technical migration; restoration; deliberate substitution.  
**Cost to inspect:** choose one version as the local “original.”  
**Transformation:** every chosen version causes the others to be labeled derivatives.  
**Residue:** a stable address with changing thumbnails.

---

## E06 — The circular sale

**Object:** a celebrated public transaction.  
**Claim:** independent demand established the work's value.  
**Anomaly:** payment passes through entities sharing infrastructure with the seller.  
**Favors:** legitimate financing; coordinated valuation; self-purchase.  
**Cost to inspect:** the monetary amount replaces adjectives in nearby testimony.  
**Transformation:** “important,” “historic” and “rare” become price figures.  
**Residue:** a receipt that has consumed its review.

---

## E07 — The cut voice note

**Object:** a short recording attributed to the artist.  
**Claim:** she refers to “the blue work.”  
**Anomaly:** the recording ends before the noun that would distinguish work, screen, test or wall.  
**Favors:** artist knowledge; unrelated studio conversation.  
**Cost to inspect:** time and acoustic attention; visual evidence fades while it plays.  
**Transformation:** repeated listening removes frequencies instead of clarifying them.  
**Residue:** breath and electrical room tone.

---

## E08 — The conservation report

**Object:** a technical report documenting a migration to newer formats.  
**Claim:** changes preserved function rather than appearance.  
**Anomaly:** the report was commissioned after the high-price sale and defines identity behaviorally.  
**Favors:** responsible conservation; replacement disguised as maintenance.  
**Cost to inspect:** exchange an image for its checksum.  
**Transformation:** visible form becomes technical equivalence.  
**Residue:** `BEHAVIOR PRESERVED / APPEARANCE NON-BINDING`.

---

## E09 — The studio receipt

**Object:** a mundane receipt for a display adapter, dated near the alleged exhibition.  
**Claim:** compatible equipment was present.  
**Anomaly:** the model number is partly obscured and could indicate an incompatible device.  
**Favors:** material possibility; anachronism.  
**Cost to inspect:** spend credibility accumulated from a witness.  
**Transformation:** zooming turns commerce into chronology.  
**Residue:** tax, timestamp, thermal-paper noise.

---

## E10 — The institutional correction

**Object:** a sequence of amended provenance statements.  
**Claim:** the institution is improving accuracy.  
**Anomaly:** each correction removes uncertainty from the public language while adding it to the internal record.  
**Favors:** scholarship; reputational laundering.  
**Cost to inspect:** the visitor must choose which version future chambers cite.  
**Transformation:** superseded language remains audible beneath the current statement.  
**Residue:** revision numbers without visible revisions.

---

## E11 — The absent witness

**Object:** references to a technician whom everyone says can resolve the case.  
**Claim:** the technician installed the work.  
**Anomaly:** contact information leads to companies, forwarding services and dead domains; no primary testimony appears.  
**Favors:** missing human witness; procedural fiction.  
**Cost to inspect:** every search adds another contact button.  
**Transformation:** the person dissolves into administrative interfaces.  
**Residue:** `DELIVERY FAILED` notices with conflicting names.

---

## E12 — The visitor's route

**Object:** the URL fragment encoding choices through the case.  
**Claim:** it documents an independent investigation.  
**Anomaly:** the route is generated inside the artwork and cites evidence the visitor altered.  
**Favors:** reproducible reading; manufactured corroboration.  
**Cost to inspect:** recognizing it changes the visitor from investigator to witness.  
**Transformation:** the route receives a catalog number and estimated value.  
**Residue:** the reader's past presented as newly discovered provenance.

---

## Evidence relations

The objects form four circuits:

### Identity circuit

E01 signature → E04 collector → E11 witness → E12 visitor.

### Material circuit

E02 photograph → E03 catalog → E05 file → E08 conservation report.

### Value circuit

E06 sale → E10 correction → E04 collector → E12 route.

### Date circuit

E02 photograph → E09 receipt → E07 voice note → E03 catalog.

No circuit closes cleanly. Each returns to a document whose authority depends on the circuit itself.

---

## Distribution rule

No session should obtain all twelve objects in readable form. It may encounter references to all of them, but direct access requires mutually exclusive payments.

Each edition privileges:

- three abundant evidences;
- three expensive evidences;
- three unstable evidences;
- two mostly absent evidences;
- one evidence that becomes the edition's apparent center.

The center is not necessarily the most probative object. It is the object the edition makes easiest to cite.

---

## Final test

Every evidence must survive three readings:

1. as a clue in a compelling investigation;
2. as a conceptual demonstration of an infrastructural limit;
3. as a literary object beautiful enough to revisit after the mystery remains unresolved.
