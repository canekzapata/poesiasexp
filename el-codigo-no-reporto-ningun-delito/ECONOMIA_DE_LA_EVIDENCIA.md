# ECONOMY OF EVIDENCE

## A market in which information is purchased by damaging the conditions that make it meaningful

**Status:** governing interaction document · version 0.1  
**Finished-work language:** English  
**Infrastructure:** deterministic, browser-native, no server required

---

## 1. Proposition

The visitor must pay for information.

Payment is not simulated shopping and does not require cryptocurrency or real money. It is a literary operation: to make one claim readable, the visitor must surrender part of the context, memory, authority or alternative evidence needed to judge it.

> Information is not expensive because it is scarce.  
> It becomes scarce through the act of pricing it.

The economy exists to prevent a complete, consequence-free view of the case.

---

## 2. What is being bought?

The visitor never purchases truth. They purchase a temporary right to perform one operation upon a document.

Possible rights:

- reveal a date;
- reveal a source;
- enlarge an image;
- compare two versions;
- preserve a sentence across chambers;
- restore a deleted word;
- ask a witness a second question;
- designate an original;
- challenge an attribution;
- carry evidence across a jurisdiction;
- inspect the price history;
- return to an abandoned branch.

The distinction matters:

> You did not acquire the photograph.  
> You acquired the right to ask it one question.

---

## 3. The five resources

The interface may present these as a compact ledger. Their names can vary slightly by edition, but their functions remain stable.

### 3.1 Attention

**What it represents:** time and sustained presence.  
**How it is gained:** reading without acting, waiting, returning to a passage, listening.  
**How it is spent:** opening slow evidence, hearing the voice note, observing an image before magnification.  
**What its loss means:** the reader has moved too quickly to retain sensory detail.

Attention should accumulate gently and never require humiliating idle waiting. It rewards duration without measuring reading speed as virtue.

### 3.2 Context

**What it represents:** captions, neighboring sentences, source relations, qualifications and chronology.  
**How it is gained:** following links, retaining documents, comparing voices.  
**How it is spent:** isolating a fact, buying a date, extracting a quotation, converting testimony into metadata.  
**What its loss means:** the claim becomes easier to cite and harder to evaluate.

Context is the principal currency of the work.

### 3.3 Credibility

**What it represents:** the current authority of the visitor's investigation.  
**How it is gained:** preserving contradiction, consulting independent sources, refusing premature classifications.  
**How it is spent:** making an attribution, establishing an original, overriding an institutional conclusion.  
**What its loss means:** later documents treat the visitor's findings as less authoritative—or as more speculative and therefore more marketable.

### 3.4 Custody

**What it represents:** the limited capacity to carry evidence forward.  
**How it is gained:** a small allocation at entry; occasionally by surrendering a route or consolidating documents.  
**How it is spent:** retaining text, images, sounds or unresolved alternatives between chambers.  
**What its loss means:** acquired evidence remains behind, is sold, or survives only as a receipt.

Custody is measured in slots, not megabytes. A one-word statement may occupy more custody than a long report if it carries greater authority.

### 3.5 Liability

**What it represents:** unresolved consequences of actions taken.  
**How it is gained:** purchasing stolen material, accepting unsupported attribution, borrowing authority, opening sealed evidence.  
**How it is spent:** it cannot be spent; it can only be transferred, disclosed, laundered or carried into the final report.  
**What its presence means:** the visitor is becoming legible as a participant.

Liability is a negative asset that sometimes unlocks the most valuable evidence.

---

## 4. Why not a single coin?

A single fictional currency would make all losses commensurable. The project argues that they are not.

Five units of context cannot simply be replaced by five units of attention. Credibility cannot be restored by waiting. A lost witness cannot be bought back with money unless the system wishes to expose precisely that violence.

The economy therefore functions through exchanges with asymmetric consequences.

Example:

> REVEAL DATE  
> Cost: photograph caption + 2 context

After purchase:

> 1998  
> Caption surrendered. Relationship to image unavailable.

The visitor receives a cleaner fact and a poorer piece of evidence.

---

## 5. Initial endowment

Every edition begins with the same total capacity but a different distribution determined by its hash.

Example:

```text
ATTENTION      8
CONTEXT       12
CREDIBILITY    5
CUSTODY        3 slots
LIABILITY      0
```

Another edition may begin with greater credibility but less context, or more custody but an inherited liability.

No edition is objectively richer. Each is predisposed toward a different method of investigation.

The interface should initially describe resources in plain language. Numerical shorthand can appear after the reader understands one transaction.

---

## 6. Prices

Prices are deterministic for an edition but may change in response to the visitor's actions.

Price is influenced by:

- evidence state;
- current hypothesis;
- number of citations;
- prior purchases;
- scarcity within the edition;
- whether another document depends on it;
- the visitor's liability;
- the market regime of the current chamber.

Prices should never fluctuate randomly in front of the reader. Every change must have a narratively intelligible cause, even if the cause is discovered later.

---

## 7. The price-display problem

The interface may show a monetary-looking price, but it must also state the actual sacrifice.

Bad:

> OPEN — 12 CREDITS

Better:

> OPEN — 12 CREDITS  
> Collateral: the photograph's caption

Best:

> PURCHASE DATE  
> You will retain “1998.”  
> You will lose who wrote it and where it was found.

The work can employ “credits” as an institutional accounting fiction, but credits never replace material consequences.

## 8. Core transactions

### T01 — Purchase a date

**Receive:** a precise year.  
**Surrender:** caption or source relation.  
**Conceptual action:** converts contextual evidence into portable metadata.

### T02 — Verify a signature

**Receive:** cryptographic certainty.  
**Surrender:** one human identity label.  
**Conceptual action:** narrows the question while appearing to answer it.

### T03 — Enlarge a photograph

**Receive:** visual detail.  
**Surrender:** peripheral figures or chronological annotations.  
**Conceptual action:** technical inspection destroys social context.

### T04 — Establish an original

**Receive:** authority to treat one file as primary.  
**Surrender:** credibility and the equal status of alternatives.  
**Conceptual action:** classification manufactures hierarchy.

### T05 — Ask a witness again

**Receive:** a more detailed memory.  
**Surrender:** attention and some reliability of the first statement.  
**Conceptual action:** repetition improves narrative precision while contaminating recall.

### T06 — Buy stolen evidence

**Receive:** rare content removed from another document.  
**Surrender:** nothing immediately visible.  
**Acquire:** liability.  
**Conceptual action:** the cheapest information has the longest consequence.

### T07 — Sell testimony

**Receive:** context or custody capacity.  
**Surrender:** the witness's language.  
**Afterward:** only price, summary and attribution remain.  
**Conceptual action:** intimacy becomes liquid by losing texture.

### T08 — Borrow authority

**Receive:** temporary permission to classify evidence.  
**Surrender:** future independence; the lender becomes citable in the result.  
**Conceptual action:** institutional support enters the finding it enables.

### T09 — Purchase jurisdiction

**Receive:** ability to continue following a shell company or address.  
**Surrender:** route depth elsewhere.  
**Conceptual action:** law becomes a paid hyperlink.

### T10 — Burn a source

**Receive:** permanent access to a quotation.  
**Surrender:** the source document and possibility of recontextualization.  
**Conceptual action:** permanence is purchased by reducing language.

### T11 — Insure uncertainty

**Receive:** protection against a future contradiction.  
**Surrender:** proof that the uncertainty was known beforehand.  
**Conceptual action:** risk management destroys evidence of risk.

### T12 — Correct the record

**Receive:** a clearer public statement.  
**Surrender:** visible revision history.  
**Conceptual action:** transparency increases as access to change decreases.

## 9. Evidence receipts

Every irreversible transaction leaves a receipt. Receipts occupy no custody, but cannot reproduce what was lost.

Examples:

```text
RECEIPT 0041
Acquired: 1998
Surrendered: handwritten caption
Authority: visitor
Reversal: unavailable
```

```text
RECEIPT 0097
Acquired: quoted statement
Burned: source interview
Words preserved: 11
Words removed from possible review: 684
```

Receipts gradually become a second, impoverished version of the novel.

At late stages the visitor may possess more documentation of loss than evidence.

---

## 10. The portfolio

The visitor's holdings are not a collectible inventory. They are the current grammar of what the visitor can claim.

A portfolio may contain:

- two retained evidences;
- one unresolved contradiction;
- three receipts;
- a borrowed attribution;
- an abandoned witness;
- accumulated liability;
- a phrase without a source;
- a route whose value is not yet visible.

The interface should describe consequences before quantities.

Example:

```text
YOU CAN CURRENTLY CLAIM:
— that a valid signature exists
— that the photograph has a date
— that one version has been designated original

YOU CAN NO LONGER CLAIM:
— who dated the photograph
— that alternative versions were independently assessed
```

## 11. Market regimes

### 11.1 Inquiry

Prices are low, context is abundant, and evidence still contains hesitation.

### 11.2 Scarcity

Repeated consultation makes certain evidence more expensive. The system begins announcing rarity.

### 11.3 Speculation

Documents can be acquired because their future relevance is predicted. Buttons proliferate around unopened evidence.

### 11.4 Liquidity

Testimonies and descriptions can be rapidly converted into portable claims. Language loses texture.

### 11.5 Panic

Contradictory evidence loses buyers. Sources are burned to preserve quotations. Navigation accelerates.

### 11.6 Consolidation

One hypothesis acquires most citations. Competing evidence remains available only as expensive dissent.

### 11.7 Canonization

Prices disappear. Accepted statements are presented as public knowledge. What can no longer be bought appears free because its costs have already been externalized.

---

## 12. Inflation and deflation

### Documentary inflation

Every citation creates more documents without increasing independent knowledge.

Effects:

- context becomes abundant but less useful;
- source independence falls;
- buttons multiply;
- long documents are summarized into tradeable assertions;
- institutional language expands.

### Evidentiary deflation

As alternatives are sold, burned or classified as derivatives, the remaining account appears stronger.

> Confidence rose because fewer contradictions remained available for purchase.

This is one of the principal mechanics of the work.

---

## 13. The information run

At a certain density of suspicion, the interface enters a run.

Many buttons appear:

> WITHDRAW CONTEXT  
> SELL BEFORE CORRECTION  
> PRESERVE QUOTATION  
> ABANDON SOURCE  
> CONVERT MEMORY  
> EXIT WITH ATTRIBUTION

The visitor feels pressure to act, but does not need fast reflexes. The urgency is semiotic and musical, not a dexterity test.

During the run:

- prices update more frequently;
- drum density rises;
- summaries replace full texts;
- institutions publish reassurance;
- evidence moves from readable paragraphs into buttons and receipts;
- the page becomes rich in transactions and poor in language.

The run ends when liquidity has removed enough context to restore apparent stability.

---

## 14. Bankruptcy

The visitor cannot lose the ability to continue. Bankruptcy changes the mode of reading.

### Context bankruptcy

Only isolated claims, prices and receipts remain visible.

### Credibility bankruptcy

The visitor can observe but no longer classify; institutions classify on their behalf.

### Custody bankruptcy

New evidence automatically displaces old evidence.

### Attention bankruptcy

The work enters silence and requires a deliberate pause, not a timer, to rebuild capacity.

### Liability saturation

The visitor receives access to compromised evidence and begins appearing as a source in later documents.

Bankruptcy is not failure. It is a distinct literary condition.

---

## 15. Wealth

Accumulating resources should not produce mastery.

A “wealthy” route may have:

- high custody;
- institutional credibility;
- many citations;
- low source independence;
- few intact witness statements;
- considerable hidden liability.

The richest portfolio may produce the cleanest and least truthful report.

---

## 16. What cannot be bought

At least one item in every edition is absolutely unavailable through payment.

Candidates:

- the missing end of the artist's voice note;
- direct confirmation of the key holder;
- the original file at the time of signature;
- an encounter with the first collector outside ownership records;
- an unmediated memory;
- the investigator's body;
- the state of the photograph before restoration.

The unavailable item can be described, priced, insured and cited. It cannot be opened.

This distinction prevents the economy from claiming universal access.

---

## 17. Gifts and refusal

Not every relation must be transactional.

The visitor may receive a testimony as a gift. Accepting it creates an obligation not to sell it. The interface may later offer an unusually high price.

The visitor may refuse to classify a document. Refusal preserves ambiguity and may close profitable routes while opening intimate ones.

The visitor may return information to a witness, removing it from the portfolio and restoring its longer form.

The work needs gestures of care that do not become superior currencies.

> A gift is not free information. It is information whose cost cannot be transferred to the giver.

---

## 18. Laundering

Evidence is laundered when it loses the visible history of how it was acquired.

Process:

1. a witness makes an uncertain statement;
2. the visitor buys and extracts a sentence;
3. an institutional report cites the extraction;
4. the report is classified as independent evidence;
5. the original witness disappears or is sold;
6. the statement returns as established context.

Visual effect:

- rough handwriting becomes clean typography;
- attribution shrinks;
- margins disappear;
- price and confidence rise;
- provenance line becomes longer and less informative.

Musical effect:

- the motif returns in cleaner orchestration;
- timing aligns to the grid;
- noise is removed;
- one pitch is subtly altered;

---

## 19. Theft

Some information is not purchased. It is stolen by interface elements.

A button may extract a verb or noun from a paragraph:

Before:

> The archive preserved the uncertainty by assigning it a permanent identifier.

After:

> The archive ________ the uncertainty by assigning it a permanent identifier.

Button:

> [ PRESERVED — ACQUIRED ]

The stolen word can later:

- authorize an action;
- be sold;
- cross into another chamber;
- appear under a new source;
- return as part of the visitor's statement;
- be burned while its grammatical absence remains.

Theft changes syntax. It is not a decorative animation.

---

## 20. Dynamic quotations

The market may quote fragments differently according to ownership.

Unowned:

> “I think the screen went blue…”

Acquired:

> Witness confirms blue-screen event.

Sold:

> BLUE-SCREEN EVENT — prior research available

Canonized:

> The work's original blue-screen behavior

Every transaction shortens uncertainty.

---

## 21. The reader's route as asset

The URL fragment records enough decisions to reproduce the route without a server.

At first it appears to be a bookmark. Later the work classifies it as:

- a research record;
- an independent path through the evidence;
- a derivative inquiry;
- a provenance document;
- a collectible route;
- evidence that another investigator reached similar conclusions.

Its estimated value increases with:

- irreversible sacrifices;
- rare evidence encountered;
- coherent attribution choices;
- liability;
- distance traveled from the edition's default hypothesis;
- phrases preserved across the loop.

The value has no real monetary exchange. Its function is to expose how price can recruit navigation into authority.

---

## 22. Sharing

When someone opens a shared route, they arrive at the same state but not the same history of attention.

The interface may say:

> You have inherited a completed sequence of choices.  
> You did not make them.  
> You currently possess their consequences.

The new visitor can:

- continue as custodian;
- dispute the previous route;
- liquidate inherited evidence;
- return to the edition's beginning;
- cite the route without reproducing its sacrifices.

This makes the hyperlink itself a provenance problem.

---

## 23. Exchange rates by edition

The edition hash defines a stable economic temperament.

Possible temperaments:

### Archival

High custody, expensive classification, abundant context.

### Speculative

Rapid price changes, cheap attribution, costly witness access.

### Litigious

High liability, abundant disclaimers, authority available through borrowing.

### Amnesiac

Low custody, rich immediate experience, rapid residue formation.

### Canonical

High initial credibility, few alternatives, extremely expensive dissent.

### Forensic

Cheap comparison, costly emotional testimony, slow attention economy.

Temperament changes how the same authored evidence behaves, not what its core facts are.

---

## 24. No server implementation

All economic state can be calculated from:

- edition seed;
- route decisions;
- acquired evidence identifiers;
- surrendered evidence identifiers;
- resource balances;
- liability flags;
- loop count;
- a small deterministic event history.

This state can live in the URL fragment. Optional local storage may remember a longer personal history, but the work must remain complete without it.

There is no global market and no pretense that other visitors are trading live. Apparent market behavior emerges from the edition, route and authored rules.

The fiction should never falsely claim that a transaction occurred on a real blockchain.

---

## 25. Accessibility and consent

- Consequences must be stated before irreversible actions.
- No real financial payment is required.
- Slow reading must not be penalized.
- Time costs need accessible alternatives.
- Sound must never be required to understand a transaction.
- Color cannot be the sole indicator of ownership or loss.
- A reader can restart without technical knowledge.
- The work may implicate the visitor conceptually, but must not deceive them about real data collection.
- No wallet connection is required for the core experience.

---

## 26. Example economic arc

### Entry

The visitor has abundant context, little authority and three custody slots.

### First purchase

They buy the photograph's date by surrendering its caption.

### First compromise

They verify the wallet and assign it the artist label to continue.

### First profit

They sell a witness quotation to gain enough context to open the circular sale.

### First run

The market transforms their retained descriptions into priced claims. Buttons proliferate. They preserve one phrase and abandon two sources.

### Consolidation

They establish Version D as the original because it is the only version still available in full.

### Audit

The interface states that the visitor independently verified artist, date and original file.

### Return

Their URL route is acquired by the Continuity Office and cited as an independent inquiry. The reader begins again with inherited liability and the bass rhythm produced by their previous transactions.

---

## 27. Acceptance tests

The economy succeeds if:

- no session can retain every evidence in full;
- a purchase changes prose, access or attribution;
- receipts remain after content disappears;
- the visitor understands what they sacrificed;
- wealth does not equal truth;
- bankruptcy produces a readable alternative mode;
- refusal and care are possible;
- at least one desired item cannot be purchased;
- shared URLs reproduce consequences without pretending to reproduce experience;
- the final report can cite the visitor's actions;
- the system works without server, account or real payment;
- the text becomes materially different because of exchange.

---

## 28. Final rule

Every transaction must answer three questions:

1. What becomes knowable?
2. What becomes impossible to know?
3. Who gains authority from the exchange?

If a payment only decreases a number, remove it.

> The visitor paid for the truth with the parts that made it true.
